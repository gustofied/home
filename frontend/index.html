    Analysis

    Floor space alone can give you the wrong impression of a facility’s capacity.

    How we compared facilities

    We sort facilities by IT floor area and split them into four groups of similar size, breaking any ties by facility code. Each bar shows the group’s median watts per square foot, so one extreme facility cannot dominate the comparison.

    We also check whether the relationship holds without the two largest markets, and without four Dallas facilities with identical area and power figures. Positive rank correlation means larger sites tend to have higher density. These comparisons do not establish why.

    04

    About the data

    Figures come from DataBank’s facility pages. We compare them with its market listings and flag disagreements. Missing information is shown as “Unknown.”

    These are advertised specifications. Some facilities may still be planned or under development, so the totals don’t tell us how much capacity is operating or available to rent.

    The data was collected on the date shown. DataBank’s published figures may be older.

    Data quality

    Missing information is concentrated, too.

    Carrier counts show how many network providers are listed at each site. They do not measure connection speed or latency.

      Calculations
      • Power density = advertised IT capacity (MW) × 1,000,000 ÷ IT floor area (ft²).
      • Median density is the middle facility value, averaging the two middle values when needed. Portfolio density divides total capacity by total floor area.
      • Capacity shares use total advertised MW; facility shares use the number of facilities.
